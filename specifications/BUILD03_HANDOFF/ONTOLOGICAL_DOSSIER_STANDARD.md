## Corpus priority
The 198-page `ONTOLOGICAL ARTIFACTS.pdf` opens with an explicit note: extant causal language is an error; the intended frame is **acausal**. Preserve original PDF untouched. Every correction must appear in `REVISION_DIFF.md` and source evidence must remain findable.

## Required sixteen sections for each dossier
1 Identity (artifact, cultural context, precise edition/original author, content version)
2 Recognition chronology (origin, operator, session, first evidence, later refinement)
3 Original thesis (verbatim with locator, claim type)
4 Native source architecture (no forced twelvefold reading)
5 Structural extraction (cardinalities, partitions, seven distance classes if justified, inversion, anchors)
6 Generative operations (what the work actually *does*, input → transformation → output)
7 Acausal correspondence (independent structures, no required influence/transmission)
8 Mathematical model (typed sets, maps, relations, examples)
9 Isomorphism/homomorphism/other correspondence specification and tests
10 Narrative temporal fidelity (events, who knew what when, source vs interpretation)
11 Primary evidence locators (Stephanus; spell; book/line; score measure; diagrams; original images)
12 Rival decompositions and uncertainty/exposure grades
13 Exact failure conditions, negative controls, correction ledger
14 Lean proof obligations and compilable excerpts where feasible
15 Cross-artifact links to prior results and linked conjectures
16 Research disposition (verified, interpretation, speculative, refuted, blocked, next task)

## Reconstruction protocol
Extract exact old premise → classify it as causal historical explanation, acausal structural interpretation, or formal mathematical relation → write corrected version without introducing a causal mechanism → preserve original passage and rationale → link source → test mathematical mapping. Acausality does not mean timestamps are interchangeable, that observational correlations establish necessity, or that a proposition is automatically a theorem.
